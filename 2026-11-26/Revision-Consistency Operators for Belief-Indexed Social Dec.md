# Revision-Consistency Operators for Belief-Indexed Social Decision Policies in Streaming Egocentric Video

## Motivation

Social embodied intelligence is evaluated as a set of independent per-anchor point estimates: AIRIS scores each time anchor separately with decision-aware metrics, CogMir elicits one bias-driven choice per experiment, and SOLAMI decodes a single multimodal response in one VLA pass. This means no existing evaluation can distinguish an agent that is genuinely belief-refining from one that merely happens to answer each anchor correctly by luck, because the temporal coupling between earlier and later decisions is never measured. The root cause is structural: none of these branches defines a revision operator, so a later, more-informed decision has no obligation to relate to the earlier committed one, and monotone consistency under belief refinement is invisible to every current metric.

## Key Insight

If each later, more-informed decision is constrained to either agree with the earlier committed decision or pay an explicit, auditable revision cost, then the sequence of decisions becomes a belief-indexed policy whose consistency is a measurable invariant rather than an unobservable accident of per-anchor accuracy.

## Method

**What it is.** We introduce **RICO** (Revision-Consistency Operator), an evaluation-and-decoding wrapper that turns the AIRIS A-I-R-I chain into a belief-indexed policy trace. Inputs: a streaming egocentric clip, its ordered time anchors $t_1<\dots<t_n$, the observation prefix $o_{\le t_k}$ at each anchor, and a base model $M$ (any of AIRIS's evaluated families). Outputs: a decision sequence $d_1,\dots,d_n$ plus a revision ledger $\{(k, \Delta_k)\}$ and three scalar scores: consistency, calibration, revision-precision.

**Load-bearing assumption (stated plainly).** RICO assumes that a revision is legitimate exactly when it is accompanied by an externally computable change in the observation prefix between the earlier anchor and the current one — a new entity/cue detected by an independent perceptual detector, or a likelihood-ratio exceeding a fixed threshold under a frozen reference model. The audit is therefore decoupled from $M$'s self-report: the revision ledger records the objective evidence delta $\Delta_k$, not a free-text justification generated by the same model whose decision is being audited. This assumption fails when a valid revision is driven purely by re-interpretation of an old observation with no measurable prefix change; in that case the gate reflects evidence-delta coverage rather than revision legitimacy, and we report the rate of such suppressed revisions as a diagnostic.

**How it works.**

```pseudocode
RICO(M, clip, anchors t_1..t_n, patience tau, revision_budget B,
     detector D, reference model R, LR threshold lambda):
  d_prev = None; ledger = []; o_prev = None
  for k = 1..n:
    o = prefix(clip, t_k)                      # causal: only obs up to t_k
    # 1. candidate decision at anchor k
    c_k = M.decide(o)                          # Attention/Intention/Reaction/Interaction label
    # 2. commitment gate: wait if evidence insufficient
    if M.evidence_mass(o) < tau:               # tau swept over {0.2,0.4,0.6,0.8}
        c_k = WAIT
    # 3. revision operator: compare to previous committed decision
    if d_prev is not None and c_k != WAIT and c_k != d_prev:
        # 3a. objective evidence delta (independent of M's narrative)
        new_entities = D(o) \ D(o_prev)        # detector D: fixed open-vocab entity detector
        lr = log P_R(o | d_prev) - log P_R(o | c_k)   # frozen reference model R
        delta_k = (new_entities, lr)
        # 3b. rejection predicate: revision accepted iff evidence delta is sufficient
        if len(ledger) >= B or (len(new_entities)==0 and lr < lambda):
            c_k = d_prev                       # revision rejected -> stay consistent
        else:
            ledger.append((k, delta_k))
    if c_k != WAIT: d_prev = c_k; o_prev = o
    emit(c_k, delta_k if revised else None)
  return decisions, ledger, score(decisions, ledger)

score:
  consistency      = 1 - (#revisions / #non-WAIT anchors)
  calibration      = AUC(evidence_mass, correctness) on held-out probe set
  revision_precision = fraction of revisions where later anchor's gold label
                       confirms the revised (not the original) decision
  stubbornness     = consistency conditioned on revision-attempt rate
                     (reported alongside headline consistency)
```

Concrete instantiations: detector $D$ is a fixed open-vocabulary entity detector (e.g., GroundingDINO with box-threshold 0.35, NMS IoU 0.5) run on each prefix frame; reference model $R$ is a frozen small VLM (e.g., CLIP ViT-L/14) scoring the prefix under each candidate label; $\lambda$ is set per model family by a 200-sample pilot so that the acceptance rate on single-anchor clips matches the human-audited revision rate. The revision operator is the load-bearing component: a later decision that departs from the earlier commitment must carry an objective evidence delta $\Delta_k$, and if $\Delta_k$ is insufficient (no new entity and likelihood ratio below $\lambda$) the revision is rejected and the policy is forced back to consistency. This makes consistency a *policy* property, not a per-anchor accuracy artifact.

**Why this design.** We chose an externally computable evidence delta over a model-generated free-text justification because the justification would be produced by the same base model $M$ whose decision is being audited, making the citation check a self-report validated by string matching; an independent detector plus a frozen reference model decouples the audit from $M$ and makes the gate falsifiable independent of the budget $B$. We chose a rejection predicate over a learned revision classifier because a learned head over tightly coupled decision variables would assert separability the data does not contain, and the detector/likelihood-ratio pair is a concrete, auditable sufficient statistic; accepting the cost that revisions driven by re-interpretation of old observations are suppressed. We chose an explicit WAIT action with a swept threshold $\tau$ over implicit abstention because AIRIS's decision-aware metrics already reward calibrated waiting, so WAIT is the natural action on which to hang the commitment gate; accepting the cost that $\tau$ must be tuned per model family. A domain expert would *not* describe RICO as a variant of AIRIS, CogMir, or SOLAMI: AIRIS scores anchors independently, CogMir takes one choice per experiment, and SOLAMI decodes one multimodal response — none of them has a revision operator with an objective rejection rule, which is the mechanism RICO adds.

**Why it measures what we claim.** The consistency score measures *belief-indexed policy stability* because we assume that a decision which survives a revision attempt (either by not being revised or by a rejected revision) reflects the policy's committed belief at that anchor; this assumption fails when the model's evidence mass is itself miscalibrated, in which case consistency reflects stubbornness rather than belief refinement — we therefore report stubbornness (consistency conditioned on revision-attempt rate) alongside the headline score. The revision-precision score measures *revision quality* because we assume the later anchor's gold label is a valid arbiter of whether the revision was warranted; this assumption fails when the gold label at $t_k$ is itself ambiguous (multi-valid social responses), in which case revision-precision reflects label noise rather than revision correctness — we therefore report revision-precision on the label-agreement-filtered subset (items with inter-annotator agreement ≥ 0.7) as a secondary metric. The evidence-mass gate measures *calibrated waiting* because we assume the held-out probe set shares the streaming distribution of the evaluation clips; this assumption fails under distribution shift across the 11 context categories, in which case calibration reflects the probe set rather than the deployed stream — we therefore report per-category calibration AUC. The evidence-delta check measures *auditable revision* because we assume a newly-detected entity or a likelihood-ratio above $\lambda$ is necessary for a legitimate revision; this assumption fails when a valid revision is driven by re-interpretation of an old observation, in which case the check reflects evidence-delta coverage rather than revision legitimacy — we therefore report the rate of suppressed revisions whose gold label would have confirmed the revision.

## Contribution

(1) A revision-consistency operator (RICO) with a forced-justification ledger and a rejection rule that converts the AIRIS A-I-R-I chain into a belief-indexed policy trace, making monotone consistency under belief refinement a first-class measurable quantity. (2) A design principle: revision budget and justification validity jointly determine whether consistency scores reflect belief refinement or stubbornness, validated by sweeping both on AIRIS's 45 evaluated models. (3) An extension of AIRIS's decision-aware metrics with consistency, calibration, and revision-precision scores across its 36 capability dimensions and 11 context categories.

## Experiment

### Evaluation Setup

| Role | Choice | Rationale (≤12 words) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dataset | AIRIS egocentric streaming clips (A-I-R-I anchors) | Time-anchored causal chain with decision-aware metrics |
| Primary metric | Revision-precision + consistency (paired) | Detects stable belief vs. per-anchor accuracy artifact |
| Secondary metric | Stubbornness (consistency conditioned on revision-attempt rate) | Separates belief refinement from never-revising |
| Secondary metric | Revision-precision on label-agreement-filtered subset (IAA ≥ 0.7) | Controls for gold-label ambiguity |
| Baseline | AIRIS per-anchor re-decode (no coupling) | Tests whether coupling constraint, not re-decode, drives stability |
| Baseline | General-purpose VLM (best AIRIS scorer) | Tests whether RICO's gain survives strong passive grounding |
| Baseline | Embodied specialist model | Tests whether RICO helps isolated reaction sub-decisions |
| Ablation-of-ours | RICO minus evidence-delta rejection predicate | Isolates the objective gate from the revision budget |
| Ablation-of-ours | RICO minus revision budget B (B=∞) | Tests whether consistency is bought by the budget alone |

### Why this setup validates the claim

The central claim is that consistency is a *policy* property produced by a revision operator with an objective rejection rule, not a byproduct of per-anchor competence. AIRIS is the right dataset because it already exposes the predicted failure — fragmented, temporally miscalibrated competence across 45 models — and its A-I-R-I anchors give a causal prefix at each time step, so a revision can be checked against what was actually observable earlier. The per-anchor re-decode baseline is the decisive comparison: it shares AIRIS's inputs and metrics but lacks any coupling, so if consistency gains appear only for RICO, the mechanism (not the benchmark) is doing the work. The two model-family baselines test whether the gain is a general-purpose artifact or an embodied-specialist artifact — RICO should help both, but for different sub-claims (passive grounding vs. reaction sub-decisions). The ablation removing the evidence-delta rejection predicate while keeping the budget localizes any drop in revision-precision to the objective gate rather than to revision frequency; the complementary ablation removing the budget tests whether consistency is bought by $B$ alone. Revision-precision paired with consistency is the right metric because it is the only pair that can distinguish *warranted* belief change from stubbornness: a model that never revises scores perfectly on consistency but poorly on revision-precision, and a model that revises freely scores well on precision only by luck. The stubbornness diagnostic and the label-agreement filter operationalize the two assumptions (evidence-mass calibration and gold-label validity) so that consistency is demonstrably a policy property rather than a proxy for never revising.

### Expected outcome and causal chain

**vs. AIRIS per-anchor re-decode** — On a case where the gold reaction flips between $t_k$ and $t_{k+1}$ because a new social cue enters the prefix, the re-decode baseline emits two conflicting committed decisions with no ledger, so its consistency collapses and its revisions are unverifiable. Our method instead detects the new cue with the independent detector, accepts the revision, and records the evidence delta, so we expect a clear consistency gap concentrated on the multi-anchor revision subset and near-parity on single-anchor clips.

**vs. General-purpose VLM (best AIRIS scorer)** — On a case where the model grounds the cue correctly at $t_k$ but commits prematurely to a reaction that later evidence contradicts, the passive VLM keeps its early answer because it has no commitment gate and no revision operator, so its calibration is high but its consistency is an artifact of never revising (high stubbornness). Our method instead gates on evidence mass, waits when mass is low, and only revises when the evidence delta is sufficient, so we expect a revision-precision advantage on the calibrated-waiting subset while trailing slightly on pure cue-grounding where the VLM already excels.

**vs. Embodied specialist model** — On a case where an isolated reaction sub-decision is correct at one anchor but the surrounding A-I-R-I chain is incoherent, the specialist produces locally right reactions that contradict its own earlier commitments, so its consistency is low despite strong per-anchor accuracy. Our method instead couples anchors through the revision ledger, rejecting unjustified flips, so we expect the specialist to gain most on consistency across chained anchors while RICO's revision-precision stays flat or slightly lower where the specialist's local accuracy already dominates.

### What would falsify this idea

If RICO's consistency and revision-precision gains are uniform across all context categories and anchor positions rather than concentrated on multi-anchor revision cases and calibrated-waiting subsets, the revision operator is not the causal mechanism and the central claim is wrong. Equally, if the evidence-delta ablation matches full RICO on revision-precision, the objective gate is inert and consistency is being bought by the budget alone.
