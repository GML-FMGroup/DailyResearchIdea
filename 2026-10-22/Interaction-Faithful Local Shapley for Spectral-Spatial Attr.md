# Interaction-Faithful Local Shapley for Spectral-Spatial Attribution

## Motivation

Existing local attribution methods such as SSI (Spectral-Spatial Interpretation) define a per-position channel game and compute exact first-order Shapley values, but they explicitly assume an additive per-channel contribution and do not establish faithfulness for interaction effects. This is a concrete instance of a broader structural gap: Shapley-value-based attribution formalisms reduce interaction-faithfulness to an additive assumption, so the resulting attribution matrix cannot recover higher-order channel interactions while preserving per-position localization. The root cause is that the local game is solved with an additive value function, which by construction assigns zero credit to any interaction that does not manifest as a main effect.

## Key Insight

Faith-Shap's polynomial least-squares approximation of the value function yields interaction coefficients that are simultaneously efficient (they sum to the local contextual contrast) and faithful (they minimize approximation error), so replacing SSI's first-order Shapley game with a Faith-Shap polynomial game at each spatial position recovers higher-order channel interactions without sacrificing the exact per-position aggregation identity.

## Method

**What it is.** Interaction-Faithful Local Shapley (IFLS) takes a hyperspectral image, a black-box classifier, and a baseline image, and produces a per-position attribution tensor of order up to K (default K=2), where each position's tensor contains first-order channel attributions and pairwise channel interaction attributions. The output aggregates exactly to the position-level contextual contrast, and the pairwise terms are faithful polynomial-approximation coefficients rather than heuristic cross terms. **Load-bearing assumption (stated explicitly):** IFLS assumes that at each position p the local game v_p(S) is well-approximated by a degree-2 polynomial in the channel indicators, so that the degree-2 Faith-Shap coefficients A_p[{c1,c2}] reflect the true pairwise channel interaction rather than the best degree-2 projection of higher-order or non-polynomial structure. When v_p contains interactions of order > 2, the degree-2 projection redistributes higher-order mass into pairwise and main-effect coefficients, and A_p[{c1,c2}] is a projection artifact (a lower bound on the true interaction). IFLS therefore reports, alongside each A_p, a measured degree-2 approximation residual (see step 5 below) so that the pairwise coefficient is interpreted as a faithful degree-2 projection with a quantified higher-order residual, not as a recovered interaction.

**How it works.**
```pseudocode
Input: image x, baseline b, model f, max order K=2, Shapley kernel weights w(S)
Output: attribution tensor A[p, c1, ..., ck] for k=1..K at each spatial position p,
        plus per-position degree-2 residual R[p] and held-out reconstruction error E[p]

for each spatial position p:
    fix surrounding context: x_ctx = x with position p replaced by b_p
    define local game v_p(S) = f(x_ctx with channels S at p set to x_p) - f(x_ctx)
    # S ranges over channel subsets; v_p(empty)=0, v_p(all)=contextual contrast
    for each subset S of channels with |S| <= K:
        compute v_p(S) by one forward pass of f
    solve the Faith-Shap weighted least-squares problem:
        minimize sum_{S} w(S) * ( v_p(S) - sum_{T subset S, 1<=|T|<=K} A_p[T] )^2
        where w(S) = (d-1) / ( C(d,|S|) * |S| * (d-|S|) )  [Shapley kernel]
        with the efficiency constraint sum_{|T|=1} A_p[T] = v_p(all)
    # closed-form normal equations with efficiency-constrained Lagrangian:
    #   unknowns theta = [A_p[{c}] for c=1..d, A_p[{c1,c2}] for c1<c2]
    #   design matrix Phi[S, T] = 1 if T subset S else 0
    #   solve (Phi^T W Phi) theta = Phi^T W v  subject to 1^T theta_main = v_p(all)
    #   via KKT system [[Phi^T W Phi, C^T],[C, 0]] [theta; lambda] = [Phi^T W v; v_p(all)]
    #   where C selects the d main-effect coordinates (C theta = sum_{|T|=1} A_p[T])
    store A_p as the order-K attribution tensor at position p
    # residual diagnostic (step 5):
    draw H=200 held-out subsets S_h not used in the fit (uniform over |S| in {1,...,d-1})
    compute R[p] = sqrt( mean_h ( v_p(S_h) - sum_{T subset S_h, |T|<=K} A_p[T] )^2 )
    compute E[p] = mean_h | v_p(S_h) - sum_{T subset S_h, |T|<=K} A_p[T] |
return A, R, E
```
The optimization is a small convex quadratic program per position; for d channels and K=2 it has d + d(d-1)/2 unknowns and is solved by the closed-form KKT normal equations above (the efficiency-constrained Lagrangian of the Faith-Shap Shapley-kernel least-squares problem). For large d, we use the sampling-based approximation from Faith-Shap: draw M=2000 subsets S with probability proportional to w(S), solve the weighted least-squares on the sampled subsets, and compute R[p], E[p] on the same H=200 held-out subsets. Runtime budget: exact enumeration at d=100 requires 2^100 subsets and is infeasible; at d<=20 (2^20 ≈ 1.05M forward passes) exact enumeration is used for validation only. For d in 100–200 the M=2000 sampling solver is used, costing 2000 forward passes per position; at 256×256 positions this is 1.31e8 forward passes per image, so positions are processed in batches of 1024 with a single batched forward pass per subset index, giving a wall-clock estimate of ~2–4 GPU-hours per HLS Burn Scar image on an A100 (measured in the experiment). Memory: the per-position design matrix for M=2000 samples and d + d(d-1)/2 ≈ 20k unknowns at d=200 is 2000×20k float32 ≈ 160 MB, held one position-batch at a time.

**Why this design.** We chose the Faith-Shap polynomial least-squares formulation over the Shapley-Taylor interaction index because Faith-Shap is the unique member of the faithful interaction index family that is both efficient and faithful under a Shapley kernel, whereas Shapley-Taylor is efficient but not faithful to the polynomial approximation; the cost is that IFLS requires solving a weighted least-squares problem per position instead of a simple weighted average. We chose to fix the surrounding context exactly as SSI does, rather than averaging over spatial coalitions as Owen-style hierarchical accounting does, because fixing the context makes the local game a well-defined function of only the channels at position p, which is what allows the per-position aggregation identity to hold exactly; the cost is that IFLS inherits SSI's assumption that the surrounding context is an appropriate conditioning variable. We chose K=2 as the default maximum order rather than a larger K because pairwise interactions capture the dominant non-additive effects in hyperspectral channel responses while keeping the number of unknowns per position at d + d(d-1)/2, which is tractable for d on the order of 100–200; the cost is that three-way and higher interactions are not explicitly recovered, which is why we report the measured degree-2 residual R[p] and held-out reconstruction error E[p] alongside A_p so that positions where the degree-2 projection is inadequate are flagged rather than silently misreported. We chose the closed-form KKT normal equations for small d and the sampling approximation for large d, rather than a single fixed solver, because the closed form gives exact coefficients when d is small enough for all subsets to be enumerated, while sampling keeps the method scalable; the cost is that the sampling approximation introduces Monte Carlo variance that the closed form does not. A domain expert would not describe IFLS as a variant of SSI because SSI solves a first-order Shapley game that is additive by construction, whereas IFLS solves a polynomial least-squares game whose coefficients are the faithful interaction indices; the two methods coincide only when all interaction coefficients are zero, which is exactly the additive case that SSI assumes and IFLS tests.

**Why it measures what we claim.** The quantity `v_p(S)` measures the local contextual contrast contributed by channel subset S because fixing the surrounding context and replacing channels S at position p is a well-defined intervention on the model input; this assumption fails when the model's response to channels at p depends on the specific values of the surrounding context in a way that is not captured by the single fixed context, in which case `v_p(S)` reflects the contrast under that particular context rather than an average contextual effect. The quantity `A_p[T]` for |T|=1 measures the first-order channel attribution because the Faith-Shap least-squares solution with the Shapley kernel is the unique efficient and faithful polynomial approximation of `v_p`; this assumption fails when `v_p` is not well-approximated by a degree-K polynomial, in which case `A_p[T]` reflects the best degree-K projection rather than the true interaction. The quantity `A_p[{c1,c2}]` measures the pairwise channel interaction because the cross term in the polynomial approximation captures the residual of `v_p` beyond the additive main effects; this assumption fails when the interaction is not symmetric or when higher-order terms absorb the pairwise effect, in which case the pairwise coefficient reflects a lower bound on the true interaction. The quantity `R[p]` (root-mean-square residual on H=200 held-out subsets) measures the degree-2 approximation quality of `v_p` because it is computed on subsets not used in the least-squares fit; this assumption fails when the held-out subsets are not representative of the Shapley-kernel distribution, in which case `R[p]` reflects sampling bias rather than approximation error. The quantity `E[p]` (mean absolute held-out reconstruction error) measures the faithfulness of the degree-2 projection to the local game because it directly compares the reconstructed polynomial value to the exact `v_p(S_h)`; this assumption fails when the held-out subsets are correlated with the fit subsets, in which case `E[p]` underestimates the true reconstruction error. The efficiency constraint `sum_{|T|=1} A_p[T] = v_p(all)` measures the position-level contextual contrast because the first-order coefficients sum exactly to the full local game value by construction; this assumption fails only if the optimization is not solved to optimality, in which case the sum reflects the residual of the solver.

## Experiment

### Evaluation Setup

| Role | Choice | Rationale (≤12 words) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dataset | HLS Burn Scar hyperspectral | Real HSI with known spatial structure and channel redundancy |
| Primary metric | Faithfulness gap (deletion AUC on contextual contrast) | Directly tests whether attributions track the local game |
| Secondary metric | Held-out reconstruction error E[p] (mean absolute error on H=200 held-out subsets) | Directly measures degree-2 approximation quality of v_p |
| Secondary metric | Degree-2 residual R[p] (RMS on H=200 held-out subsets) | Quantifies higher-order mass not captured by K=2 |
| Baseline | MC-Owen estimator | Tests whether fixed-context game beats coalition-averaged accounting |
| Baseline | SSI (first-order Shapley) | Tests whether pairwise faithful terms recover missed interaction |
| Baseline | Shapley-Taylor (K=2) | Tests whether Faith-Shap kernel beats efficient-but-unfaithful index |
| Ablation-of-ours | IFLS with K=1 (additive-only) | Isolates contribution of pairwise faithful coefficients |

### Why this setup validates the claim

The central claim is that IFLS's per-position Faith-Shap coefficients are faithful to the local contextual game and that the pairwise terms capture non-additive channel effects that first-order methods miss. Each baseline isolates one sub-claim. MC-Owen tests whether fixing the surrounding context (rather than averaging over spatial coalitions) yields attributions that better match the exact local game value; if the fixed-context game is the right conditioning variable, IFLS should show a smaller faithfulness gap where Owen's averaging smears the contrast. SSI tests the additive assumption: since SSI's first-order game is additive by construction, any position where pairwise interactions are nonzero should be systematically misattributed, and IFLS should close that gap. Shapley-Taylor tests the kernel choice: it is efficient but not faithful to the polynomial approximation, so on positions with strong interactions its coefficients should deviate from the least-squares optimum and IFLS should track the contextual contrast more tightly. The K=1 ablation of IFLS removes the pairwise terms while keeping the fixed-context game and Faith-Shap kernel, so any gain attributable to interaction recovery is isolated from the game formulation itself. The faithfulness-gap metric is the right detector because it measures whether the attribution reconstructs the actual model response to channel interventions, which is exactly the property the design claims; accuracy-style metrics would not distinguish faithful from coincidentally predictive attributions. The secondary metrics E[p] and R[p] directly measure the degree-2 approximation quality of v_p on held-out subsets, so the interaction-faithfulness claim is grounded in measured approximation error rather than asserted: positions with large R[p] are reported as degree-2 projections with a quantified higher-order residual, and the comparison of E[p] across IFLS, SSI, Shapley-Taylor, and the K=1 ablation tests whether the Faith-Shap kernel yields a lower reconstruction error than the alternatives. The deletion-AUC metric is interpreted as a faithfulness proxy under the stated assumption that deletion order under the baseline corresponds to the Shapley-kernel coalition weighting; this assumption fails when correlated channels make deletion non-interventional, in which case deletion AUC reflects a correlated-channel artifact rather than local-game faithfulness, which is why E[p] is reported as the primary direct faithfulness measure and deletion AUC as a secondary proxy.

### Expected outcome and causal chain

**vs. MC-Owen estimator** — On a case where a burn-scar boundary pixel has channels at position p that interact with a spatially varying neighborhood, MC-Owen averages the game over sampled spatial coalitions, producing attributions that reflect an average context rather than the actual one, so its reconstruction of the specific contextual contrast is off. Our method fixes the surrounding context exactly, so the local game is a well-defined function of only the channels at p and the efficiency constraint holds exactly, so we expect a noticeably smaller faithfulness gap and smaller held-out reconstruction error E[p] concentrated on boundary and heterogeneous-neighborhood positions, with near-parity on homogeneous interior pixels.

**vs. SSI (first-order Shapley)** — On a case where two adjacent spectral channels jointly drive the classifier response (e.g., a water-absorption pair), SSI's additive first-order game assigns each channel a marginal share and cannot represent the joint effect, so its attributions sum to the contextual contrast but distribute it wrongly across the pair. Our method solves the degree-2 Faith-Shap least-squares problem, so the pairwise coefficient absorbs the residual beyond main effects, and we expect a visible gap in E[p] on positions where the pairwise interaction magnitude is large, with parity where interactions are near zero.

**vs. Shapley-Taylor (K=2)** — On a case where the local game has a strong non-additive component, Shapley-Taylor's weighted-average coefficients are efficient but not faithful to the polynomial approximation, so its pairwise terms deviate from the least-squares optimum and its reconstruction of v_p(S) for intermediate subsets is biased. Our method uses the Shapley-kernel weighted least-squares solution, which is the unique efficient and faithful polynomial approximation, so we expect IFLS to have lower held-out reconstruction error E[p] on intermediate subsets and to show a faithfulness-gap advantage that grows with interaction strength.

**vs. IFLS with K=1 (ablation)** — On a case where pairwise interactions are present, the K=1 ablation forces all non-additive structure into first-order terms, so its attributions are efficient but misallocate credit between interacting channels. Our full K=2 method recovers the pairwise coefficients, so we expect the ablation to match IFLS on additive positions but to show a clear deficit in E[p] and in the faithfulness gap on interaction-heavy positions, confirming the pairwise terms are doing the claimed work.

### What would falsify this idea

If IFLS's faithfulness-gap advantage over SSI and Shapley-Taylor is uniform across positions rather than concentrated where pairwise interaction magnitude is large, or if the held-out reconstruction error E[p] is not lower for IFLS than for the baselines on interaction-heavy positions, then the pairwise faithful coefficients are not the causal driver and the central claim fails. Likewise, if the K=1 ablation matches full IFLS everywhere, or if the measured degree-2 residual R[p] is large at the positions where IFLS claims an advantage, the interaction-recovery claim is unsupported.
