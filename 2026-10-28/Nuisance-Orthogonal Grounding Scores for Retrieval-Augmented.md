# Nuisance-Orthogonal Grounding Scores for Retrieval-Augmented Agents

## Motivation

Across both lineages of RAG evaluation, the reported grounding signal is contaminated by nuisance state that is not part of the query-evidence relation: InfoDeepSeek's agentic metrics co-vary with web-state drift because the benchmark treats the live page as authoritative, and CausalRAG's retrieval quality co-varies with LLM-extraction variance because the extracted causal graph is treated as fixed ground truth. Structurally, neither work separates the query-conditioned signal from environment/extractor state, so scores are not stable functions of the query-evidence pair and cross-run comparisons are meaningless.

## Key Insight

The query-evidence relation is invariant under the group generated by web-state drift and LLM-extraction transformations, so projecting the raw grounding statistic onto the orthogonal complement of that group's nuisance tangent space yields a score that depends only on the query-evidence pair and is unchanged by any nuisance realization.

## Method

**What it is.** NOGS (Nuisance-Orthogonal Grounding Score) takes a query q, a set of observed evidence passages e = {e_1..e_k} produced by a retriever+extractor pipeline, and a nuisance-perturbation ensemble {g_1..g_M} drawn from the transformation group G (web-state drift, extraction re-sampling). It outputs a scalar s(q,e) ∈ ℝ that is invariant to any g ∈ G applied to the evidence.

**Load-bearing assumption (stated explicitly).** NOGS assumes that the 8-dimensional descriptor φ(r) = [drift_descriptor(r), extractor_entropy(r)] is a sufficient statistic for the first-order action of G on the frozen reader's answer logit a_r, i.e., that the entire first-order effect of web-state drift and extractor resampling on a_r lies in the span of the M=16 sampled perturbation directions Φ, so that βᵀt absorbs all nuisance variation and leaves s(q,e) invariant. This assumption is not asserted as exact; it is measured in Phase 5 (residual leakage diagnostic) and reported as a quantity, and the method's invariance claim is scoped to the first-order component captured by φ.

**How it works.**

```pseudocode
INPUT: query q, observed evidence e, perturbation ensemble size M=16,
       group generators G = {web_drift, extractor_resample}, ridge λ=1e-3,
       descriptor dim d=8, held-out perturbation count H=8

PHASE 1 — nuisance ensemble
  for m in 1..M:
      g_m ~ G                       # one web-drift OR extractor-resample draw
      e^(m) = g_m(e)                # perturbed evidence realization
  for h in 1..H:                    # held-out perturbations, NOT used in fit
      g_h ~ G
      e^(h) = g_h(e)

PHASE 2 — raw grounding statistic
  for each realization r in {e, e^(1)..e^(M), e^(1)..e^(H)}:
      a_r = A(q, r)                 # A = answer/entailment logit from frozen reader
  a_bar = mean(a_r over the M+1 fit realizations)

PHASE 3 — nuisance tangent estimation
  # features φ(r) = [drift_descriptor(r), extractor_entropy(r)]  (dim d=8)
  Φ = [φ(e^(1))-φ(e); ...; φ(e^(M))-φ(e)]      # M×d perturbation directions
  Δa = [a_(1)-a_bar; ...; a_(M)-a_bar]           # M×1 statistic deviations
  β = (ΦᵀΦ + λI)^{-1} Φᵀ Δa                      # nuisance regression coeffs

PHASE 4 — orthogonalization
  t = φ(e) - mean_g φ(g(e))                      # observed nuisance tangent
  s(q,e) = a_e - βᵀ t                            # project out nuisance component

PHASE 5 — residual leakage diagnostic (reported, not used in s)
  for h in 1..H:
      s_h = a_(h) - βᵀ (φ(e^(h)) - mean_g φ(g(e)))
      resid_h = s_h - s(q,e)
  report Var(resid_h) and mean |resid_h| as functions of perturbation magnitude
  fit second-order term: regress resid_h on [φ(e^(h))-φ(e)]_i [φ(e^(h))-φ(e)]_j
  for all i≤j (d(d+1)/2 = 36 features) with ridge λ=1e-3; report R² of this fit
```

The invariance is exact in the population limit under the stated load-bearing assumption: for any g ∈ G, φ(g(e))−φ(e) lies in the span of Φ, so βᵀt absorbs the entire first-order change in a, leaving s unchanged. The ridge term λ=1e-3 stabilizes when M < d. Phase 5 measures the residual that the first-order model leaves; if that residual is large, the invariance claim is scoped to the first-order component and the residual is reported rather than assumed zero.

**Why this design.** We chose an explicit finite perturbation ensemble over a learned invariance head because the group generators (web drift, extraction resampling) are physically realizable transformations we can apply to evidence without a model, so the nuisance tangent is estimated from data rather than asserted — accepting the cost that M=16 forward passes per query are required. We chose a linear nuisance regression in the descriptor φ over a nonlinear invariance penalty because the group action on the answer logit is locally linear in the drift/extraction descriptors (the first-order term dominates for realistic perturbation magnitudes), accepting the cost that second-order interactions are unmodeled — and Phase 5 quantifies exactly this unmodeled second-order component. We chose the frozen reader's entailment logit as the raw statistic over a fine-tuned grounding head because fine-tuning would re-introduce extractor-specific bias, accepting the cost that the raw statistic is noisier and relies on the orthogonalization to remove nuisance. A domain expert would not describe NOGS as a variant of standard RAG reranking (e.g., Atlas's retriever scoring) because reranking optimizes query-document similarity, whereas NOGS computes a projection onto the orthogonal complement of a symmetry group's tangent space — an operation retrieval cannot express since it has no notion of the nuisance ensemble.

**Why it measures what we claim.** The computational quantity a_e (raw answer logit on observed evidence) measures query-conditioned grounding strength because the frozen reader's entailment logit is monotone in the degree to which evidence supports the answer; this assumption fails when the reader hallucinates support for a plausible but unsupported answer, in which case a_e reflects reader prior rather than grounding. The quantity βᵀt (nuisance projection) measures the component of the grounding statistic attributable to environment/extractor state because φ is a sufficient descriptor of the first-order group action on evidence; this assumption fails when the drift or extraction variance is not captured by the 8-dimensional descriptor or when the action is not locally linear, in which case residual nuisance remains in s — and Phase 5 reports the magnitude of this residual. The quantity s(q,e) = a_e − βᵀt measures reproducibility-invariant grounding because the group-invariance of the projection makes s identical across any two realizations e, g(e) that differ only by a first-order nuisance transformation; this assumption fails when the perturbation ensemble under-samples the group (M too small relative to descriptor dimension) or when nuisance directions lie outside span(Φ), in which case s retains variance under unobserved nuisance directions, again measured by Phase 5.

## Contribution

(1) NOGS, a nuisance-orthogonalization operator that produces a grounding score invariant to web-state drift and LLM-extraction variance by projecting the raw grounding statistic onto the orthogonal complement of the transformation group's tangent space. (2) A design principle: grounding scores for RAG agents should be defined as functions on the quotient of evidence space by the environment/extractor symmetry group, not on raw evidence, which yields cross-run comparability as a structural property rather than a benchmark patch. (3) A controlled-perturbation protocol for measuring score variance under nuisance transformations, applicable to existing benchmark data from InfoDeepSeek and CausalRAG without re-collection.

## Experiment

### Evaluation Setup

| Role | Choice | Rationale (≤12 words) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dataset | InfoDeepSeek dynamic-web queries, replayed against a cached snapshot with a reproducible synthetic drift generator (HTML table shuffling, boilerplate injection, timestamp perturbation) | Real drift semantics, exactly reproducible perturbations |
| Primary metric | Grounding-score stability under re-retrieval: Var(s(g(e)) − s(e)) over held-out perturbations g | Directly tests invariance, not just answer accuracy |
| Secondary metric | Residual leakage: Var(resid_h) and mean \|resid_h\| from Phase 5, plus R² of the second-order fit | Quantifies how much invariance the linear model leaves |
| Secondary metric | Answer accuracy and calibration (ECE) on held-out queries using NOGS-ranked evidence | Tests downstream impact beyond stability |
| Baseline | Standard RAG reranking (Atlas-style) | Tests whether nuisance projection beats similarity scoring |
| Baseline | Un-orthogonalized reader logit | Tests whether projection itself drives the gain |
| Baseline | Ensemble-averaged logit (mean over M) | Tests whether explicit tangent projection beats naive averaging |
| Ablation-of-ours | NOGS with M=2, d=8 descriptors | Tests whether ensemble under-samples the group tangent space |
| Ablation-of-ours | NOGS with H=8 held-out perturbations excluded from fit | Tests residual leakage on unseen nuisance directions |

### Why this setup validates the claim

The central claim is that NOGS's score is invariant to web-state drift and extractor resampling, and that this invariance is what makes grounding strength reproducible. InfoDeepSeek is the right dataset because its queries are answered against a live, drifting web, so the same query yields genuinely different evidence realizations across retrievals — exactly the nuisance group G that NOGS claims to quotient out. Replacing uncontrolled live drift with a reproducible synthetic drift generator applied to a cached snapshot makes the perturbation ensemble exactly reproducible, so the M=2 ablation and the held-out-perturbation residual test are meaningful rather than confounded by drift non-stationarity. The primary metric, stability of the grounding score across re-retrievals of the same query, is the quantity the invariance claim is *about*; accuracy alone could rise for reasons unrelated to nuisance removal. The secondary residual-leakage metric directly measures the load-bearing assumption: if Var(resid_h) is large on held-out perturbations, the 8-dimensional descriptor is not sufficient and the invariance claim is scoped to the first-order component. The three baselines each isolate a different sub-claim: reranking tests whether a retrieval-side score can achieve the same stability (we predict it cannot, since it has no nuisance ensemble), the raw un-orthogonalized logit tests whether projection contributes anything beyond the reader, and the ensemble mean tests whether the *tangent projection* specifically beats a simpler variance-reduction heuristic. The M=2 ablation tests the stated failure condition — whether too few perturbations leave residual nuisance variance — so if the full method's stability advantage disappears at M=2, the mechanism is confirmed rather than incidental. The downstream accuracy/calibration metric tests whether the stability gain matters for the task, and the held-out-perturbation residual test bounds the sufficiency of φ.

### Expected outcome and causal chain

**vs. Standard RAG reranking (Atlas-style)** — On a case where a passage's extractor re-samples a table with slightly different cell ordering between retrievals, the reranker produces a similarity score that shifts with surface form, so its grounding estimate is unstable across re-retrievals because it has no notion of a nuisance group. Our method instead estimates the drift tangent from the perturbation ensemble and projects it out, so we expect a clear stability gap on drift-heavy subsets (tables, multi-column pages) but near-parity on clean prose where drift is minimal. We also expect NOGS-ranked evidence to yield higher answer accuracy and lower ECE than reranker-ranked evidence on the same held-out queries, since stability should translate into more reliable grounding.

**vs. Un-orthogonalized reader logit** — On a case where the frozen reader's entailment logit rises purely because a re-extracted passage happens to include a redundant supporting sentence, the raw logit inflates without genuine grounding improvement because it conflates evidence content with extractor state. Our method subtracts the βᵀt nuisance component, so we expect the raw logit to show high cross-retrieval variance that our score removes, with the gap concentrated on passages whose extractor entropy descriptor varies most. The Phase 5 residual leakage on held-out perturbations is expected to be small relative to the raw-logit variance, confirming that the first-order projection captures most of the nuisance.

**vs. Ensemble-averaged logit (mean over M)** — On a case where drift is asymmetric (one realization drifts far, others near), simple averaging is pulled by the outlier realization because averaging reduces variance without identifying the nuisance direction. Our method regresses the statistic on the descriptor and projects onto the orthogonal complement, so we expect averaging to close only part of the stability gap while NOGS closes most of it, with the residual gap largest when the perturbation directions are anisotropic. The second-order fit in Phase 5 is expected to have low R² on clean prose but non-trivial R² on drift-heavy tables, quantifying the unmodeled nonlinear component.

### What would falsify this idea

If NOGS's stability gain over the ensemble-mean baseline were uniform across all subsets rather than concentrated on high-drift, high-extractor-entropy passages, the tangent-projection mechanism would not be doing the work and the central claim would be wrong. Equivalently, if the M=2 ablation matched the full method's stability, the ensemble would be unnecessary and the invariance would be incidental. Additionally, if Phase 5's held-out residual Var(resid_h) were comparable to the raw-logit variance on drift-heavy subsets, the 8-dimensional descriptor would be insufficient and the invariance claim would be falsified for those subsets.
