# Contrastive Conditional Score Distillation under Random Pairing

## Motivation

Random-conditioning distillation (e.g., the Random Conditioning method) replaces matched image-caption pairs with random condition-image pairing, but the marginal score-matching loss is invariant to permutations of condition-score assignments, so the student's per-condition score is only weakly constrained. This identifiability hole is structural across image-free distillation pipelines (DKDM, DMD) that assume marginal matching transfers conditional knowledge; as a result, the student can satisfy the loss while misassigning scores to conditions, especially for rare or unseen concepts.

## Key Insight

A contrastive, condition-indexed score-matching objective whose minimizer is the teacher's conditional score for every condition in the support is identifiable under random pairing because the contrast between the sampled condition and perturbed negative conditions breaks the permutation invariance of marginal matching.

## Method

**What it is.** We introduce Condition-Contrastive Score Distillation (CCSD), a distillation loss that trains a student conditional diffusion model to match the teacher's score under random condition-image pairing. Inputs: a pretrained teacher ε_θ(x_t, t, c), a student ε_φ(x_t, t, c), a noised image x_t (from teacher rollouts or noise), and a condition c sampled from the condition distribution (not matched to x_t). Output: student parameters φ updated by a contrastive score-matching loss that identifies the per-condition score.

**Load-bearing assumption (stated explicitly).** The contrastive term provides a per-condition identification signal only if the teacher's conditional score function is sufficiently separated across conditions: for the sampled (x_t, c) and each negative condition c_k, the teacher score separation Δ_k = ||ε_θ(x_t, t, c) - ε_θ(x_t, t, c_k)||^2 must be large enough that the contrastive logits are not near-uniform. This assumption fails when the perturbation is too weak (e.g., dropping a stopword) or when c has no near neighbors in the condition space; in those cases the contrastive term contributes noise rather than identification. We therefore (i) gate each negative by its measured teacher separation and (ii) select negatives adaptively per (x_t, c) to maximize separation, as specified below.

**How it works.**
```pseudocode
# One training step of CCSD
sample t ~ U[0,1], noise ε ~ N(0,I)
sample condition c ~ p(c)                      # random, not matched to image
sample x0 from teacher rollout or noise; x_t = α_t x0 + σ_t ε
# Positive score: teacher under the sampled condition
s_pos = ε_θ(x_t, t, c)
# Negative scores: teacher under K=4 perturbed/other conditions
# Adaptive selection: from a candidate pool of M=16 conditions (8 token-dropout
# perturbations of c with dropout rate 30%, 8 random samples from p(c)),
# compute teacher scores and keep the K=4 with largest Δ_k = ||s_pos - s_neg_k||^2.
for k in 1..K:
    c_k = selected negative condition
    s_neg_k = ε_θ(x_t, t, c_k)
    Δ_k = ||s_pos - s_neg_k||^2
    w_k = 1[Δ_k > δ]                           # gate: δ = 0.1 * mean(Δ over pool)
# Student scores under the same conditions
s_stu_pos = ε_φ(x_t, t, c)
# Contrastive score-matching: student must match teacher's positive score
# and be closer to teacher's positive score than to teacher's negative scores
L_pos = ||s_stu_pos - s_pos||^2
L_contrast = -log( exp(-||s_stu_pos - s_pos||^2 / τ) /
                   ( exp(-||s_stu_pos - s_pos||^2 / τ) +
                     Σ_k w_k * exp(-||s_stu_pos - s_neg_k||^2 / τ) ) )
L = L_pos + λ * L_contrast                     # λ=0.1, τ=0.1
update φ by ∇_φ L
```
The positive term is standard score matching under the sampled condition. The contrastive term forces the student's score under c to be closer to the teacher's score under c than to the teacher's scores under perturbed conditions, creating a per-condition identification signal. Negatives are generated by token dropout (masking 30% of condition tokens) and by sampling other conditions from p(c); no real images or matched captions are needed. The gate w_k zeroes out negatives whose teacher separation Δ_k is below δ, so uninformative negatives (near-identical teacher scores) contribute no gradient.

**Why this design.** We chose a contrastive objective over plain marginal score matching because marginal matching is permutation-invariant across conditions, so it cannot distinguish a student that assigns the correct score to the wrong condition; the contrastive term breaks this invariance by explicitly comparing the positive condition against negatives, accepting the cost that each step requires M+1 teacher forward passes (M=16 candidate pool, K=4 selected). We chose teacher-generated negatives (perturbed conditions) over student-generated negatives because the teacher's scores under perturbed conditions are stable and provide a fixed reference ordering, accepting the cost that negatives are not adaptive to the student's current errors. We chose a fixed temperature τ=0.1 and λ=0.1 over learned weighting because the contrastive loss scale is set by the score-matching residual, and tuning these on a small validation set avoids introducing a second optimization loop; the trade-off is that the balance between positive matching and contrastive identification is not instance-adaptive. A domain expert would not describe this as a variant of DMD or DKDM because those methods match marginal scores or trajectories without any per-condition contrast; the contrastive term is the load-bearing mechanism that provides identifiability, not an auxiliary regularizer.

**Why it measures what we claim.** The contrastive residual ||s_stu_pos - s_pos||^2 versus ||s_stu_pos - s_neg_k||^2 measures per-condition score identifiability because we assume that the teacher's score under a perturbed condition is sufficiently different from its score under the true condition (a separation assumption on the teacher's conditional score function, quantified by the gate threshold δ); this assumption fails when the perturbation is too weak (e.g., dropping a stopword), in which case the negative score is nearly identical to the positive and the contrastive term reflects noise rather than identification. The negative sampling distribution measures coverage of the condition space because we assume that sampled conditions and token-dropout perturbations span the support of p(c); this assumption fails for extremely rare conditions with no near neighbors, in which case the contrastive term provides no signal for those conditions. The temperature τ measures the sharpness of the identification signal because we assume the score-matching residuals are on a comparable scale across conditions; this assumption fails when some conditions have much larger residual variance, in which case τ effectively downweights those conditions.

## Contribution

(1) A condition-contrastive score distillation loss (CCSD) that replaces marginal score matching with a contrastive, condition-indexed objective, requiring no real images or matched captions. (2) A design principle for constructing negatives via token dropout and condition resampling that creates a per-condition identification signal without additional teacher training. (3) An empirical demonstration that contrastive conditioning improves generation quality for rare and unseen concepts under random pairing compared to marginal distillation.

## Experiment

### Evaluation Setup

| Role | Choice | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dataset | ImageNet-1k with class-conditional teacher | Rich condition space to test identifiability |
| Primary metric | Condition-adherence under shuffled prompts | Directly probes per-condition identification |
| Secondary metric | Misassignment rate: fraction of generated samples whose classifier score is higher for a swapped condition than for the given condition | Quantifies the magnitude of the identifiability problem |
| Baseline | Random Conditioning distillation | Tests whether random pairing alone suffices |
| Baseline | DMD2 | Tests marginal score matching without contrast |
| Baseline | DKDM | Tests data-free distillation without contrast |
| Ablation-of-ours | CCSD with λ=0 (positive term only) | Isolates contrastive term's contribution |
| Ablation-of-ours | CCSD with fixed token-dropout negatives (no adaptive selection, no gate) | Tests the contribution of adaptive negative selection and gating |

### Why this setup validates the claim

The central claim is that marginal score matching is permutation-invariant across conditions, so distillation cannot identify which score belongs to which condition, and that a contrastive term fixes this. ImageNet-1k with a class-conditional teacher gives a large, structured condition space where mis-assignment is measurable: we evaluate by generating with shuffled or swapped prompts and checking whether the student's output follows the prompt it was actually given versus the prompt it should have matched. Random Conditioning tests the sub-claim that random pairing alone does not buy identifiability. DMD2 tests the sub-claim that marginal distribution matching, even when strong on sample quality and speed, leaves the condition-identification gap open. DKDM tests whether data-free distillation without contrast suffers the same gap. The λ=0 ablation is the load-bearing control: if removing the contrastive term does not degrade condition-adherence while leaving sample quality similar, the contrastive term is not doing the work we claim. The fixed-negatives ablation tests whether the adaptive selection and gating (which rely on the separation assumption) are necessary for the contrastive term to provide signal. Condition-adherence under shuffled prompts is the right metric because it isolates the per-condition assignment from overall fidelity; a method could have excellent FID and still fail this test, which is exactly the failure we predict for the baselines. The misassignment rate quantifies how often the student's output is better explained by a swapped condition than by the given one, providing a direct measure of the identifiability gap.

### Expected outcome and causal chain

**vs. Random Conditioning distillation** — On a case where the student is trained with randomly paired images and captions, the baseline produces a student that assigns roughly the correct marginal score but blurs condition-specific detail, because random pairing gives no signal distinguishing which condition the score belongs to. Our method instead trains the student to match the teacher's positive score while pushing it away from teacher scores under perturbed conditions, because the contrastive term supplies the missing per-condition identification signal. We expect a noticeable gap in condition-adherence under shuffled prompts, with parity on unconditional sample quality, and a lower misassignment rate for CCSD.

**vs. DMD2** — On a case where two conditions have similar marginal score statistics but different per-condition means, the baseline produces a student that matches the marginal distribution yet occasionally generates the wrong condition's content, because DMD matches marginal scores and trajectories without any per-condition contrast. Our method instead reproduces the teacher's ordering of positive versus negative condition scores, because the contrastive residual explicitly compares the positive condition against negatives. We expect a concentrated gap on near-neighbor condition pairs, with small or no gap on well-separated conditions, and a lower misassignment rate for CCSD on near-neighbor pairs.

**vs. DKDM** — On a case where the student must acquire a concept absent from matched image-caption pairs, the baseline produces a student that captures the general data distribution but fails to bind the concept to its condition, because data-free distillation without contrast has no per-condition reference. Our method instead anchors each condition to the teacher's score under that condition via the positive term and separates it from perturbed conditions via the contrastive term, because negatives are teacher-generated and stable. We expect a moderate gap on rare and near-neighbor conditions, with parity on common, well-separated ones, and a lower misassignment rate for CCSD on rare conditions.

**vs. CCSD with fixed token-dropout negatives (no adaptive selection, no gate)** — On a case where the perturbation is weak (e.g., dropping a stopword), the fixed-negatives variant produces a contrastive term with near-uniform logits because the teacher's score under the perturbed condition is nearly identical to the positive, so the gradient is dominated by the positive term. Our method instead selects negatives with the largest teacher separation and gates out those below δ, because the adaptive selection and gating directly address the separation assumption. We expect the fixed-negatives variant to perform similarly to the λ=0 ablation on condition-adherence, while full CCSD shows a clear gain, confirming that the separation assumption is load-bearing and that our repair is necessary.

### What would falsify this idea

If the λ=0 ablation matches full CCSD on condition-adherence under shuffled prompts, or if the gain over DMD2 is uniform across condition subsets rather than concentrated on near-neighbor and rare conditions where the predicted identification failure occurs, or if the fixed-negatives ablation matches full CCSD (indicating the separation assumption is not the bottleneck), then the contrastive term is not providing identifiability and the central claim is wrong.
