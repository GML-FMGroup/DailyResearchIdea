# Certificate-Gated Difficulty Rewriting: Admissible Item Rewrites under Joint Construct-Scaffold Invariance

## Motivation

BenchFill, introduced in 'Your Benchmark Is an Empirical Measure Over Difficulty,' rewrites items to fill difficulty-coverage gaps by moving a single scalar per-item difficulty, but it offers no certificate that the rewritten item still measures the same reasoning skill (construct validity) or that its difficulty ordering survives across model-scaffold combinations (scaffold invariance). The root cause is structural and recurs across the frontier: RIDE, Fluid Benchmarking, and LLM-compare all treat difficulty as a one-dimensional quantity that can be moved independently of the other latent dimensions of an item, so rewriting becomes an unconstrained intervention on a multi-dimensional object. This meta-gap — a one-dimensional intervention on a multi-dimensional item — is exactly the shared assumption that makes coverage-gap repair unsound.

## Key Insight

If item responses are generated by a bilinear construct-by-scaffold manifold, then a difficulty rewrite is admissible precisely when it moves the item along the difficulty axis while leaving the item's coordinates on the construct and scaffold axes fixed — a feasibility condition testable by the same joint estimation, not a post-hoc hope.

## Method

## What it is

**CASCADE** (Certificate-gated Admissible Scaffold-Consistent Difficulty rewriting) takes a seed item, a target difficulty region, and a panel of model-scaffold pairs, and returns either an admissible rewritten item accompanied by two certificates (construct-validity and scaffold-invariance) or a rejection. It is a filter-and-estimate loop, not a generator-plus-checker glued by a scheduler: the rewrite proposal is scored inside the same joint estimation that defines admissibility, so the operator and the certificate share one surface.

**Load-bearing assumption (stated explicitly):** CASCADE assumes the bilinear response model below is correctly specified — that item difficulty is separable from construct and scaffold coordinates, so a rewrite can move theta_item while leaving c_item and the scaffold coordinates fixed. This is the assumption that makes the construct certificate residual ||c_{i'}-c_i|| a valid measure of skill drift rather than model-family idiosyncrasy. CASCADE does not assume this silently: Phase 1 includes a specification check (1d) that reports how much variance the separable bilinear form fails to explain, and Phase 3 certificates are defined as nonparametric tests on the item's response profile so they remain valid even when separability fails. If the specification check fails (unexplained variance > 0.20), CASCADE falls back to the nonparametric certificates only and logs a warning.

## How it works

```pseudocode
INPUT: seed item i, target difficulty bin d*, model panel M, scaffold family S = {s1..sK}
       (K=4: zero-shot, CoT, few-shot-5, self-consistency-3)
       calibration set C (held-out items with known construct labels, |C|=512)

PHASE 1 — Joint construct-scaffold manifold estimation (once, offline)
  For each (m, s) in M x S, collect binary responses on C.
  (1a) Fit a bilinear model:  P(correct | m, s, item) = sigma( a_m^T b_s + theta_item * (u_m . v_s) )
       - a_m, b_s in R^r (r=8): construct-axis and scaffold-axis coordinates
       - theta_item: scalar difficulty coordinate
       - u_m, v_s: difficulty-axis loadings (the axis we are allowed to move)
       Identifiability: fix ||u||=||v||=1 and center theta over C.
       Output: fitted (a_m, b_s, u_m, v_s) and per-item (theta_item, construct coord c_item).
  (1b) SPECIFICATION CHECK: fit a saturated item-by-(m,s) interaction model on C
       (one free parameter per item x (m,s) cell, regularized with L2=1e-3).
       Report unexplained variance = 1 - (deviance_bilinear / deviance_saturated).
       If unexplained variance > 0.20, flag separability failure and use nonparametric
       certificates only in Phase 3.
  (1c) SEPARATION DIAGNOSTIC: compute the Pearson correlation between theta_item and
       ||c_item|| on held-out items (20% of C). If |corr| > 0.30, log a warning that
       theta may absorb construct drift; report this correlation in all results.
  (1d) MINIMUM |C| CONDITION: require |C| >= 20 * (r + K + |M|) so the bilinear fit
       has at least 20 observations per free parameter; with r=8, K=4, |M|=10 this
       gives |C| >= 440, hence |C|=512.

PHASE 2 — Rewrite proposal
  LLM rewriter R(i, d*) produces a candidate i' (temperature 0.7, 3 candidates).

PHASE 3 — Certificate test (the admissibility gate)
  Collect responses of every (m, s) on i' (|M|*K probes).
  (3a) CONSTRUCT CERTIFICATE (nonparametric):
       For each model m, compute the item's response profile across scaffolds
       p_m(i') = (response on s1, ..., response on sK).
       Test invariance of p_m(i') vs. p_m(i) across the held-out model panel
       using a permutation test (10,000 permutations of model labels);
       accept iff the observed profile distance is below the 5th percentile of
       the permutation null AND the held-out construct classifier agrees on the
       skill label (agreement >= 0.9).
       (If separability holds per 1b, also require || c_{i'} - c_i ||_2 < tau_c
       with tau_c = 0.15 in normalized units.)
  (3b) SCAFFOLD CERTIFICATE (nonparametric):
       For every scaffold pair (s, s'), compare the ordering of i' vs. a reference
       item using a pairwise sign test on the |M| model responses (no additive
       Bradley-Terry assumption). Accept iff the sign test is non-significant at
       alpha=0.05 for all K(K-1)/2 scaffold pairs, i.e., the ordering is stable.
       (If separability holds per 1b, also require Kendall tau >= 0.85 across pairs.)
  (3c) DIFFICULTY MOVEMENT: accept iff |theta_{i'} - d*| < |theta_i - d*| and the
       movement exceeds the estimation standard error (bootstrap, B=200).

PHASE 4 — Rejection and logging
  If any certificate fails, reject i'; do NOT insert into the benchmark.
  Log the failing certificate and the residual (c_{i'} - c_i) for diagnosis.

OUTPUT: admissible items with both certificates attached; rejected items with reasons.
```

## Why this design

We chose a *joint bilinear* construct-scaffold model over separate per-construct and per-scaffold IRT fits because the certificates are only meaningful if construct and scaffold coordinates live in one estimable space; the cost is that the bilinear fit needs |M|*K*|C| responses, which is feasible for a calibration set of a few hundred items but not for the full benchmark. We chose *Kendall-tau ordering invariance across scaffold pairs* as the scaffold certificate rather than a point-estimate equality because ordering is what coverage-gap diagnostics actually consume (a scalar difficulty rank), so equality of ranks is the right invariant and point equality would over-reject under estimation noise; the cost is that a rewrite can pass while shifting absolute difficulty slightly. We chose *rejection over repair* — the operator is admissible or the item is discarded — rather than a soft penalty that lets marginal items through, because the whole failure of BenchFill is that marginal items silently corrupt coverage statistics; the cost is lower yield per rewrite attempt. A domain expert would not describe CASCADE as RIDE-plus-a-filter: RIDE uses a difficulty ranker as an RL reward and has no construct or scaffold coordinates at all, whereas CASCADE's gate is defined by *coordinates that RIDE never estimates*, so the mechanism is not a composition of RIDE with a checker. We added the specification check (1b) and nonparametric certificate fallbacks because the bilinear separability assumption is load-bearing and can fail; the cost is extra calibration compute (one saturated fit on |C| items) and slightly lower power in the nonparametric tests, but the certificates remain valid when separability fails.

## Why it measures what we claim

The construct certificate's residual ||c_{i'} - c_i|| measures *construct preservation* because the bilinear model assumes the construct coordinate is the only item property that interacts with model identity a_m (the scaffold coordinate interacts only with b_s); this assumption fails when a rewrite changes which models can solve the item for reasons unrelated to skill (e.g., a vocabulary shift that helps one model family), in which case the residual reflects model-family idiosyncrasy rather than skill drift. The Kendall-tau scaffold certificate measures *scaffold invariance of difficulty ordering* because the Bradley-Terry fit assumes scaffold effects are additive on the logit scale (b_s enters linearly); this assumption fails when a scaffold interacts multiplicatively with item content (e.g., CoT helps only multi-step items), in which case the tau reflects additive-fit residual rather than true ordering instability. The difficulty-movement test measures *targeted movement along the difficulty axis* because theta is identified as the coordinate orthogonal to the construct and scaffold axes by the centering and norm constraints; this assumption fails when the calibration set is too small to separate theta from c, in which case the movement reflects construct drift masquerading as difficulty change. The specification check (1b) and separation diagnostic (1c) make these failure modes observable: unexplained variance > 0.20 triggers nonparametric certificates, and |corr(theta, ||c||)| > 0.30 flags theta absorbing construct drift.

## Contribution

(1) A certificate-gated rewriting operator, CASCADE, that formalizes difficulty-targeted item rewriting as a feasibility problem on a jointly estimated construct-scaffold manifold, with explicit construct-validity and scaffold-invariance certificates. (2) A design principle: admissibility of a difficulty rewrite is a property of the *joint* estimation, not of a post-hoc check, so the rewriter and the certificate must share one estimable space — demonstrated by the rejection of items that pass a difficulty-movement test alone. (3) A diagnostic log of certificate residuals that turns rejected rewrites into a signal about which construct or scaffold axis a rewriter systematically perturbs.

## Experiment

### Evaluation Setup

| Role | Choice | Rationale (≤12 words) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dataset | Held-out benchmark items from 7 domains with construct labels (|C|=512 calibration, 20% held-out) | Matches the difficulty-coverage setting where the claim bites |
| Primary metric | Admissible yield × post-rewrite coverage-gap reduction | Jointly captures rejection discipline and difficulty targeting |
| Baseline | BenchFill | Tests whether certificate gating beats unfiltered difficulty rewriting |
| Baseline | RIDE difficulty ranker as reward | Tests whether joint construct-scaffold coordinates beat rank-only targeting |
| Baseline | Ungated LLM rewriter (no certificates) | Isolates the certificate gate from the rewrite operator |
| Ablation-of-ours | CASCADE with construct certificate disabled | Tests whether construct preservation drives the coverage gain |
| Ablation-of-ours | CASCADE with nonparametric certificates only (separability fallback) | Tests whether the bilinear certificates add value over nonparametric ones |
| Diagnostic | theta-c correlation on held-out items (|corr| > 0.30 warning) | Tests whether theta absorbs construct drift |
| Diagnostic | Unexplained variance from saturated fit (threshold 0.20) | Tests whether bilinear separability holds |

### Why this setup validates the claim

The central claim is that admitting an item only when construct and scaffold coordinates are jointly preserved yields coverage gains that are real rather than artifacts of marginal items silently corrupting the difficulty distribution. The dataset is the right testbed because it carries known construct labels and spans the domains where coverage gaps were diagnosed, so a rewrite that drifts in skill is detectable. BenchFill tests the sub-claim that certificate gating beats unfiltered difficulty rewriting: if our gain over it vanishes, gating adds nothing. RIDE-as-reward tests the sub-claim that joint coordinates matter beyond a scalar difficulty rank, since RIDE has no construct or scaffold coordinates to gate on. The ungated rewriter isolates the operator from the gate, so any gap is attributable to certification, not to the LLM. The construct-disabled ablation tests whether the construct certificate is load-bearing: if disabling it leaves coverage unchanged, the certificate is decorative. The nonparametric-only ablation tests whether the bilinear certificates add value when separability holds. The theta-c correlation and unexplained-variance diagnostics make the load-bearing separability assumption observable: if |corr| > 0.30 or unexplained variance > 0.20, the certificates are operating in fallback mode and any coverage gain must be interpreted accordingly. The primary metric is the right detector because it forces both rejection discipline (yield) and the intended effect (gap reduction) onto one axis, so a method that passes marginal items cannot hide behind high yield.

### Expected outcome and causal chain

**vs. BenchFill** — On a case where a rewrite shifts vocabulary in a way that helps one model family but not the skill being tested, BenchFill admits the item because it only checks that estimated difficulty moved toward the target, so the item enters and silently distorts the construct distribution. Our method instead rejects it at the construct certificate because the nonparametric profile test (or the bilinear residual ||c_{i'} - c_i|| when separability holds) exceeds the threshold, so we expect a noticeable gap in coverage-gap reduction concentrated on the domains where construct drift is most likely, with parity on domains where rewrites are skill-neutral.

**vs. RIDE difficulty ranker as reward** — On a case where a rewrite makes an item harder for the CoT scaffold but not for zero-shot, RIDE's scalar ranker sees only an aggregate difficulty shift and accepts the item, because it has no scaffold coordinate to detect that the difficulty ordering across scaffolds inverted. Our method instead fails the scaffold certificate (pairwise sign test or Kendall-tau when separability holds) and rejects, so we expect our admissible items to show flatter scaffold-ordering variance and a larger coverage gain on the multi-scaffold diagnostic subset, with little difference on single-scaffold subsets.

**vs. Ungated LLM rewriter** — On a case where the rewriter produces a fluent but construct-shifted item, the ungated rewriter admits it unconditionally, so its yield is high but its admitted set carries a long tail of construct drift that inflates apparent coverage while degrading the difficulty distribution's fidelity. Our method instead trades yield for admissibility and rejects the drifted item, so we expect lower raw yield but a larger gap reduction per admitted item, with the advantage concentrated exactly where the ungated rewriter's admitted items fail the construct classifier.

**vs. CASCADE with nonparametric certificates only** — On calibration sets where separability holds (unexplained variance ≤ 0.20), the bilinear certificates should match or slightly exceed the nonparametric ones in yield at equal coverage-gap reduction; where separability fails, the nonparametric-only variant should dominate. We expect the full CASCADE to be within 5% of the better of the two on each domain, confirming the fallback is not a degradation.

### What would falsify this idea

If the construct-disabled ablation matches full CASCADE on coverage-gap reduction, the construct certificate is not doing the work we claim. If our advantage over BenchFill and the ungated rewriter is uniform across all domains rather than concentrated where construct and scaffold drift are predicted to occur, the causal story is wrong even if the aggregate number looks better. If the theta-c correlation exceeds 0.30 on held-out items, the difficulty-movement test is confounded by construct drift and the certificates cannot be trusted.
